# The session open

Every session after the first opens on what they already hold, before it adds anything. Four
steps, in order. All four run in a drill as well as a course: a drill has a ledger the moment it
has promoted anything. New material is step 4, and it starts only once step 3 has produced an answer
you can judge.

## 1. Read the ledger

Read `NOTES.md`, `02 - Areas/<Topic>/Learning Log.md`, and list `04 - Permanent/<Topic>/`. The
notes say what they hold, the log says how firmly and how recently, and together they are the
zone of proximal development.

*Done when:* you can name every concept they have been promoted on, and the date of each.

## 2. Find what is **due**

Each log entry carries a `**Review:**` date. Anything on or past that date is due.

Set the next one from how the recall actually went, which is expanding retrieval: the gap grows
while they keep succeeding and collapses the moment they fail.

| What happened at recall | Next review |
|---|---|
| Produced it cold, unprompted | Double the last gap, starting at one week |
| Produced it after a hint | Repeat the last gap unchanged |
| Could not produce it | One day, and the concept returns to a lesson |

Gaps of days-to-weeks are doing the work here; the exact number matters far less than the
expansion, so do not tune it. When several concepts are due at once, take them in one mixed
set rather than one at a time - **interleaving** is what forces them to select the right idea
rather than run a primed one.

*Done when:* you have the due list, or have established it is empty.

## 3. Open **cold**

Ask for the due concept from memory, with nothing on screen. Their own note stays closed:
opening it converts retrieval into recognition and the value is gone.

Take a **confidence** rating first - high, medium, low - then the answer. The gap between the
two is the diagnostic the rest of the session runs on:

- **High confidence, wrong.** Stop here and teach this. A confident wrong model is the highest
  value thing you will find, and it predicts where they will stumble on everything adjacent.
  Log it as a misconception in flight.
- **Low confidence, right.** They hold it and do not trust it. Say so, and stretch the gap.
- **Confidence tracking accuracy.** Their self-assessment is calibrated. Trust their read on
  what to teach next.

A cold open is also where the evidence for promotion comes from, so run it even when nothing is
due and you are only checking a claim from last session.

*Done when:* they have committed an answer and a confidence rating, and you have judged both.

## 4. Then teach

Pick the most mission-relevant thing inside their zone of proximal development: challenging
enough to cost effort, close enough to reach. When they name the thing they want, teach that
instead.

With no mission, what they asked for and the zone of proximal development are the whole selection
rule. A drill has no compass beyond the thing in front of them, which is the point of it.

Where step 3 exposed a confident wrong model, teach that first regardless of the mission.

*Done when:* a lesson exists on disk and they have been pointed at it.
